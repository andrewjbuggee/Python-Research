------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TO DISCUSS 1: iEEI and OHC reference in the CAM6 column (current J04 convention kept)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Every 2015-onward run is integrated from 2015 and shifted up by the WACCM6 historical run's 2014
  annual-mean iEEI and OHC (J04's "preceding_case").
- There is no CAM6 historical run in our data, so the CAM6 column starts from WACCM6's 1850-2014
  accumulation. Its 2015-2069 changes are CAM6's own; only the starting level is borrowed.
- Alternatives:
  (i)  measure iEEI/OHC from 2015 in every column, so no model configurations are mixed;
  (ii) keep 1850 for WACCM6 and the model, start CAM6 at 0 in 2015 with its own y-range;
  (iii) find or run a CAM6 historical simulation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TO DISCUSS 2: is CMIP6-baseline.000 the parent of the CMIP6 MCB runs?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Consistent with yes: the baseline covers 2015-2069 and the four MCB runs start in Jan 2035. The
  file names give the same compset (BSSP245cmip6) and grid (f09_g17).
- Chen et al. (2024, GRL, doi:10.1029/2024GL108860) used CESM2/CAM6 at 1.25 x 0.9 deg: a baseline
  for 2015-2034 under SSP2-4.5, then MCB runs for 2035-2069. Their five MCB runs are named by seeded
  area (5% most; 10-40% least susceptible), not 025/050/075/125PCT, so ours may be a different set.
- Their Table 1 control: 288.8 K (2020-2039) and 289.8 K (2050-2069). Our baseline's global TS:
  289.85 K and 290.84 K, so about 1.05 K warmer in both periods but with the same ~1.0 K warming. Our
  WACCM6 SSP2-4.5 mean is also ~1 K above their value (289.75 K for 2020-2039). This is inconclusive;
  a different temperature variable or averaging is possible (my guess, unverified).
- Question for Jonah: where do the CMIP6-MCB-xxxPCT runs come from, and what does "xxxPCT" mean?
